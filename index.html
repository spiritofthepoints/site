Why I Made This

A Personal Offering

For more than twenty years, I have lived inside the questions that Taoism, yoga, and meditation ask of us. How do we stay open when life breaks us? How do we move with what is, rather than against it? How do we remain present to our own lives?

When I trained as an acupuncturist, I found that Chinese Medicine was asking the same questions — only it had mapped the answers into the body itself. Into a system of living intelligence that had been listening to what it means to be human for thousands of years.

I began writing this deck while sitting beside my father as he was dying.

There were days when I didn't know how to be in that room and not fall apart completely. The grief would rise and I had nowhere to put it. I felt the particular helplessness of loving someone you cannot help.

During those weeks, certain acupuncture points kept coming to me — Spirit Storehouse, Gate of Hope, Wail of Grief. They became quiet companions — not answers, but presences. They showed me how to stay open when everything in me wanted to close. How to keep offering my heart even when it ached.

Point by point, I began to write my way through loss.

What started as a personal practice became this. The Spirit of the Points held me close to my own center when everything else was shifting. Now it is my heart's offering to you.

— Suzanne Spiegel, Author

What Makes This Different

The Wisdom Moves Deeper
When the Mind Follows

Over the years in my practice I began to guide my patients into meditation while the needles rested in their points. Not a lecture about Chinese Medicine, not an explanation of what the points do — but a gentle, spacious invitation into the living quality — the spirit — of the treatment itself.

The result is a quality of change that goes deeper than needles alone. The meditation allows the mind to attune to the shift the body is already making. The needles open the door. The meditation helps you walk through it.

The Spirit of the Points system carries that same potency.

Each card activates a point — a quality of attention, an ancient intelligence, a particular way of meeting your life. The guided meditation that accompanies every card is designed to do what meditation does in my treatment room: help you drop in. To move from reading about the wisdom to actually feeling it move through you.

Taoist wisdom was never meant to be studied. It was meant to be lived — in the body, in the breath, in the texture of ordinary days.

That’s what this system makes possible — not just for the people who can lie on my table, but for anyone ready to go deeper than a card alone can take them.

One Hundred Meetings

Bai Hui

You are bigger than the chaos or challenge before you.

There is a vast intelligence, Tian Qi or Heavenly Qi, that moves through all things. It holds the potential of the cosmos, linking you to the rhythm of nature and the unfolding of life itself. The Governing Vessel, which travels along the spine and opens at the crown, is a celestial channel that receives this immense intelligence.

When you rise above the story of “me,” even for a breath, you tap into this current, and it changes everything.

Consider the scale of your existence. You are a speck on a planet spinning at sixty-seven thousand miles per hour, orbiting a star, shooting through the Milky Way, hurtling through an endless sea of galaxies. And yet, here you are, breathing, alive, precisely where you are meant to be.

From this height, fixation begins to loosen. Petty fears and daily frustrations shrink against the backdrop of eternity. You are not small. You are the universe expressing itself in human form, and that intelligence moves through everything you touch.

This is not a call to abandon the human world. It is an invitation to hold it all — the intimate and the vast, the dishes and the stars — with reverence. They are not separate.

When you widen into this view, the mind loosens, the heart opens, and wisdom rushes in. What once felt like pressure becomes presence.

Tian Qi has always been here, flowing through you, waiting for you to notice. Lift your gaze. Breathe it in. Let the vastness steady you.

Remembering the Big Picture

When stress or small worries take hold, pause. Put down your phone, step outside, and look up. Notice the sky, its color, its movement, its scale. Let it draw your awareness higher. Feel the space above you, around you, and within you.

Breathe it in. Then take a minute to envision yourself exactly where you are: in this place, in this moment, on this spinning planet, orbiting a star, moving through the Milky Way, carried within an endless sea of galaxies.

Let this vast perspective become the context for your day. Carry it with you.

Yin Valley

Yin Gu

Soften into slowness. Savor the ordinary miracle of being alive.

Life’s most precious gifts cannot be chased or forced. They reveal themselves when you slow down enough to receive.

In a world that glorifies more — more doing, more striving, more accumulation — it is easy to forget what you came here for. But the faster you move, the more you miss. What you are truly seeking is not out ahead of you. It is already here, inside this moment.

There’s always an opening to pause. To listen. To feel your own heartbeat.

Slowness may feel uncomfortable at first. Sitting beside a lake, doing nothing, you might feel restless, your mind reaching for distraction. That’s okay. Let it come. Let it pass.

In Chinese medicine, anxiety and restlessness often arise when the Kidneys are depleted. Returning to stillness begins to restore them. As the mind-body settles, those reserves gather again.

Let discomfort flutter through you like snow falling onto an open, quiet field. In staying, the breath deepens, tension softens, anchored presence returns.

When you stop chasing the next moment and return to this one, you remember why you are here. Not to perform. Not to perfect. But to feel. To marvel. To love. To be.

Through the Valley

Fu Tong Gu

Life moves in cycles. Yin to Yang. Light to dark. Inhale to exhale. This constant interplay shapes your existence.

Periods of joy give way to edges: loneliness, anxiety, grief. Not as disruptions, but as part of life’s natural rhythm.

Just as dawn gives way to dusk, yang’s outward reach is always followed by yin’s return inward, calling you home to yourself.

There’s a reflex to push discomfort away. To tense against sadness. To reject what feels too raw. But when you soften — when you let the waves rise and fall without resistance — something shifts.

You begin to cultivate the kind of compassion that can hold pain without judgment. Water teaches this.

Winter is the season of depth, stillness, and emotional truth. The Kidneys hold your capacity to be with what’s hard. They whisper: you don’t have to fix this. You only have to stay.

Stay with what’s raw. Stay with what’s rising. And let it move, like a tide returning to the sea.

When you make room for what once felt unbearable, expansion returns. Joy blooms through the grace of allowing.

Daily Ritual
In Compassion

Today, let compassion guide the way you meet yourself and the world.

When difficult emotions or situations arise, pause. Feel into your body and ask: What would it feel like to meet this moment with an open, friendly heart, rather than with judgment or resistance?

You don’t need to change anything. Simply notice what shifts in you when you soften.

At the end of the day, take a few minutes to journal: How did this shift the tone of your thoughts, emotions, or interactions? What did you notice about yourself or others when compassion was at the forefront?
